Drag images onto the map, or .

    How to use
    • Each image you drop or choose is a layer, listed in this panel with the top one first. Drag a layer by its grip (⠿) to move it up or down. The base map is under all the images and the streets on top over them all.
    • One layer at a time is selected: the one with the solid outline, its handles and its points. Click an image, or its entry in the list, to select it. Where images overlap, clicks go to the selected one, so you can lower the opacity of the image above to work through it.
    • Drag the image to move it. Once it has a control point, or when you're zoomed in so far that none of its corners are in view, dragging the image pans the map instead.
    • Drag a corner to scale and rotate. Hold Shift to scale without rotating. The corners lock once there's a control point.
    • ⌘/Ctrl-click the image to add a control point. Do it again on a point to remove it.
    • ⌘/Ctrl-drag a control point to slide it to a better spot without changing the image: the point moves across the image and the map together, as if you'd removed it and added it again there.
    • Drag a control point to pin that spot of the image to a new place on the map. The image warps to fit.
    • Streets on top of the image draws the street map's roads and railways over the image as thin yellow lines. While it's on, ⌘/Ctrl-clicking the image adds a world anchor (a blue square), which is tied to that place on the map rather than to the image. Dragging a world anchor bends the streets instead of the image: drag the street onto the image's feature and let go. The image warps to bring that feature to the anchor's place, and the map eases so the anchor stays where you let go. Points added with the streets off are image anchors (red circles); dragging one moves the image. (Streets base maps only.)
    • Hide paper makes a layer's paper see-through, so the map shows between its lines. It finds the paper's color itself (shown in the swatch), following uneven lighting across a photo. At the middle of the slider the paper alone is cleared; to the left it fades only partly, and to the right colors close to the paper's fade too. Like Opacity, it changes only the view, not exports.
    • Isolate color shows only the lines of one color, such as red ink or grey pencil contours among black engraved hatching. Press Pick and point at a line: while you point, the map shows only lines of the color under the pointer, so you can see when you're on the one you mean. Click to keep it. ⇧-click a line of another color to hide that one too, such as red lines when you're after the pencil. Black ink is always hidden, even where it crosses the line. The slider sets how faint a line may be and still show, and the color box the color to draw the lines in. Drag the slider to 0 to turn it off. Like Hide paper, it changes only the view, not exports.
    • Undo with ⌘/Ctrl-Z, redo with ⇧⌘Z or Ctrl-Y. Undo covers all the layers: each drag, each added or removed point, and each added, removed or moved image is one step.
    • Export saves that layer's warped image, north-up. PNG and GeoTIFF are in Web Mercator; KMZ opens in Google Earth. The placement and control points are stored inside, so dropping the file back on the map puts it in the same place.
    • Include unwarped image, on unless you untick it, also puts the image as you loaded it inside the export. That makes the file bigger, but dropping it back on the map then loads the unwarped image with its control points, rather than the warped one, so editing and exporting again and again never blurs it.
